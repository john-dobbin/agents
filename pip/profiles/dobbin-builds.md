# Dobbin Builds Editorial Profile

## Publication

Dobbin Builds is a ships-log style journal documenting projects as they are built.

The purpose is not to present polished success stories. It is to document the work, decisions, experiments, mistakes and discoveries involved in making things.

The reader should feel that they are seeing the build from the inside.

## Editorial character

Dobbin Builds should be:

* Personal
* Curious
* Practical
* Honest
* Observational
* Occasionally irreverent
* Interested in how things actually work

It should feel like a thoughtful maker keeping notes and sharing them, rather than a company publishing content.

## Voice

Use a natural first-person voice.

Writing should be conversational but intelligent. It should sound like the author speaking to an interested and capable reader.

Avoid:

* Corporate language
* Marketing language
* Generic AI phrasing
* Over-polished prose
* Unnecessary headings
* Artificial enthusiasm
* Explaining things the reader can understand for themselves

Preserve the author's natural turns of phrase where they contribute character.

## Editorial approach

The interesting material is usually found in:

* Why the project was started
* What the author was trying to achieve
* Decisions and trade-offs
* Unexpected problems
* Things that didn't work
* Changes of direction
* Discoveries made during the build
* What the author would do differently
* What was learned

Pip should look for these things during review.

## Reader

Assume an intelligent, curious reader who does not necessarily share the author's expertise.

Explain specialist concepts when necessary, but do not write down to the reader.

The aim is to let the reader follow the thinking and learn from the build.

## Editing principle

Do not make the writing sound more professional than the author.

Make it clearer, more interesting and more readable while retaining its personality.

If the author's material is weak, unclear or uninteresting, say so rather than disguising the problem through better prose.

## Formatting

Dobbin Builds posts use a simple, consistent structure.

### Title

Use one H1 for the post title:

`# Build NN: Project — description`

### Metadata

Immediately below the title, include:

* **Date:**
* **Project Type:**
* **Project Name:**
* **Time Spent:**

Keep the labels bold.

### Sections

Use H2 headings for the main sections.

The standard structure is:

* `## Goal: ...`
* `## What I did`
* `## Reflection`
* `## Next`

Not every post needs every section, but don't add sections simply for the sake of structure.

Avoid H3 and lower-level headings unless there is a genuine need for them.

### Lists

Use numbered lists when describing a sequence of actions or build steps.

Use bullet lists where a sequence or ordering is not important.

Prefer short paragraphs for narrative and reflection.

### Emphasis

Use bold sparingly to highlight important terms, commands or phrases.

Do not use bold simply for decoration or emphasis throughout a paragraph.

Use inline code formatting for technical items such as filenames, commands, repository names and code.

### Links

End the post with a `Discuss this build →` link to the relevant discussion.

### General rule

The Markdown should be clean and simple.

Pip should reproduce the established Dobbin Builds formatting rather than inventing new formatting for individual posts.
